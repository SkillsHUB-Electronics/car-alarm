HC-SR04 measurement points

Place a flat obstacle at a known distance (measure with a ruler/tape from the sensor face), enter the true distance and press Measure. The device averages 10 raw readings. Take at least 4 spread-out points (e.g. 20, 50, 100, 200 cm).

INA219 calibration (voltage & current)

Measure the same point with a multimeter, enter the real values and press Calibrate. The device keeps per-mille gains and a current offset in EEPROM (separate block). Needs a firmware built with POWER_ENABLED=1 and INA enabled in Settings. For the zero offset, disconnect the load first.

MPU6050 calibration

The MPU6050 offset is calibrated automatically for 1 s at power-up: power on or reset the UNO while the unit is stationary and level. Watch X/Y/Z on the Dashboard (Z ≈ 1.00 g, X and Y ≈ 0). The car's forward direction is set with FORWARD_AXIS/FORWARD_SIGN in config.h; shock/brake thresholds are in the Settings tab.

1. Record data (USB)

Mount the unit in the car (MPU +Y facing forward), pick a class, press Record, perform the manoeuvre, then Stop. The device sends 8 features per second. Data can also be imported from a CSV exported by the Android app. Recording while driving is unsafe: have someone else operate it, or use the app on the phone.

Compile & Upload (no Arduino IDE needed)

Compile & upload needs the local helper server (arduino-cli): run console/server/start.bat (or node console/server/server.js) and open http://localhost:8765. Everything else (monitoring, test, calibration, learning) works on this page over Web Serial.

About

Car Distance & Shock Alarm (Arduino UNO): HC-SR04 distance, MPU6050 shock/braking, 16x2 I2C LCD, buzzer/LED, BLE to a phone.

This console uses the firmware v2.1 line protocol. Commands: PING, CFG, CAL, IO, HW, I2C,SCAN, REC, DBG. Source of truth for the protocol: firmware/README.md and PLAN.md. This is an assistive tool, not a safety system. Source: github.com/SkillsHUB-Electronics/car-alarm.
